🏔️ Issyk: Tragedy and Gold

In October, when I was in Almaty, a friend and her family took me to Lake Issyk. Turquoise water, golden birches, mountains in a haze. The beauty is unbelievable. And behind it is a story that takes your breath away.

🌊 The tragedy

Until July 7, 1963, Issyk was called the emerald of the mountains: a hotel, boat docks, weekend buses from Alma-Ata. On that hot Sunday, meltwater from the Zharsai glacier burst through a moraine dam. Around two in the afternoon the first wave of the mudflow came down the gorge, and there were twelve in all: mud, boulders, uprooted trees. The lake’s natural dam gave way, and Issyk was almost drained. The town below was hit too.

Ivan Aushev, the director of the Issyk tourist base, walked the shore with a megaphone warning people, and then ferried them by boat to safer places.

In 1963 the newspapers didn’t print a word about the dead, and official figures were never published. Memories put the number of victims anywhere from 50 to 2,000. You can read more about that day and see archival photos in Qalam’s “From Issyk to Medeu”.

🕊️ Legends of the gorge

People also tell two legends about that day.

The first is about an old shepherd. He barely spoke Russian and didn’t even know the word for “mudflow”. He ran to the people by the lake shouting in Kazakh: “Ketińder! Qara lai kele jatyr!” — “Leave! Black mud is coming!” No one understood him. He couldn’t read, but he read the mountains better than anyone. He felt the danger before people did, and so did his sheep, his horse and his dog.

The second is about the pioneers. There was a children’s summer camp nearby, and that day a group of schoolchildren set off on a hike. Their trail led to where the mudflow later hit hardest. But after a while, three of the children suddenly felt unwell. No one knows why. The group stopped, and that may have saved them all.

Who stopped them? Maybe the same dove of cloud was flying over the gorge that day too.

👑 The Gold Discovery

Six years passed. In 1968 Issyk became a town, and a vehicle depot was planned at its entrance. Under Soviet law protecting monuments, archaeologists had to check the land first, and on the building site stood a six-meter burial mound. The excavation was led by Beken Nurmukhanbetov; Kemal Akishev made the description and the reconstruction. The central tomb had been robbed in ancient times, but Nurmukhanbetov asked to clear a patch slightly to the side, and there they found an untouched wooden burial chamber.

Inside lay a young Saka warrior, about 16–18 years old, dressed in more than 4,000 gold pieces, with a tall pointed headdress of about 70 centimeters and a silver bowl with an inscription of 26 mysterious characters.

✨ Today

The “Golden Man”, Altyn Adam, became a symbol of Kazakhstan. His figure on a winged snow leopard crowns the Independence Monument in Almaty.

And the lake came back. By the early 1990s the dam was restored, and today Issyk is turquoise again. I stood on its shore and thought about everything it has lived through. And that day it seemed even the angels were happy: they sent us a dove of cloud 🕊️☁️

Behind unexpected shocks and hard blows there are often opportunities no one saw coming. We just have to look closely.
